# Community Pain Mining

## Overview

Find repeated pain in the words customers use. Community pain becomes useful when it points to urgency, budget, repeated failure, or a workflow people already try to solve.

## What To Capture

- exact phrases and recurring wording
- painful triggers and moments of failure
- current workarounds
- tools people mention
- what they tried and abandoned
- objections, distrust, and buying barriers
- signs of budget, paid tools, consultants, or urgent business impact

## Scoring

Score each pain from 1 to 5:

| Factor | What 5 Means |
| --- | --- |
| Frequency | Many independent mentions across communities |
| Urgency | People need a solution soon |
| Money proximity | Pain blocks revenue, saves labor, or reduces cost |
| Buyer clarity | The person with the pain can buy or influence budget |
| AI fit | AI can solve or accelerate the workflow |

## Workflow

1. Define the audience and communities to inspect.
2. Search for complaint, workaround, and tool-comparison language.
3. Group repeated pains by job-to-be-done.
4. Preserve language that can become hooks, landing copy, and sales questions.
5. Separate monetizable pain from general frustration.
6. Send strong pains to `paid-intent-research` and `ai-solution-blueprint`.

## Output

| Pain Theme | Exact Language Pattern | Audience | Current Workaround | Monetization Clue | AI App Hypothesis | Score |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

End with:

- top 5 phrases to reuse in content or sales
- strongest monetizable pain
- what evidence is still missing
- next community/search source to inspect

## Common Mistakes

- Summarizing pain too cleanly and losing customer language.
- Treating loud complaints as demand.
- Ignoring whether the complaining person can pay.
- Forgetting to look for workarounds and alternatives.
