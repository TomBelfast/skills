# Paid AI Niche Discovery

## Overview

Find niches where people already spend money or have a strong business reason to pay. Interest is not enough. Search volume, social chatter, and trendiness are only useful after paid intent is visible.

Use this as the orchestrator for the full niche research workflow. Use the focused skills when a step needs deeper work:

- `paid-intent-research`
- `keyword-demand-research`
- `community-pain-mining`
- `competitor-offer-analysis`
- `poland-market-adaptation`
- `ai-solution-blueprint`

## Core Rule

Paid intent is the gate. A niche with weak payment proof cannot be `build`, even if it has strong content potential.

## Research Flow

1. Define the candidate buyer, problem, and outcome in one sentence.
2. Find global proof that money already moves in the niche.
3. Map keyword demand and community pain as supporting evidence.
4. Analyze existing offers and alternatives.
5. Adapt the opportunity to Poland.
6. Convert strong niches into an AI app or service blueprint.
7. Decide `build`, `test`, `watch`, or `reject`.

## Scoring

Score each category from 1 to 5:

| Category | What 5 Means |
| --- | --- |
| Paid intent proof | Clear spend: SaaS, agencies, ads, jobs, budgets, pricing, consultants, or services |
| Money proximity | Problem directly creates revenue, saves labor, or prevents losses |
| Buyer access | Buyer can be reached through YouTube, Skool, social, outbound, partners, or communities |
| AI app feasibility | AI can produce a useful workflow or product without fragile custom work |
| YouTube potential | The problem creates compelling Polish AI content and demos |
| Skool potential | The niche benefits from community, templates, workshops, or peer learning |
| Poland adaptation | Local language, trust, pricing, and SMB maturity make the opportunity plausible |
| Competition risk | Low score means commoditized; high score means there is a defensible wedge |

Decision thresholds:

- `build`: paid intent 4-5, total average 4+, clear Polish wedge.
- `test`: paid intent 3+, one missing proof point can be validated quickly.
- `watch`: interesting but weak payment proof or unclear buyer access.
- `reject`: no hard paid-intent evidence, weak buyer, or poor AI fit.

## Output Contract

Always end with this table:

| Niche | Buyer | Problem | Proof They Pay | Existing Solutions | Gap | AI App To Build | YouTube Angle | Lead Magnet | Paid Offer | Score | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

After the table, write:

- top 3 assumptions to validate
- first validation test
- strongest reason not to build

## Common Mistakes

- Treating search volume as paid intent.
- Listing ideas without naming the buyer.
- Ignoring cheaper alternatives, agencies, consultants, spreadsheets, or manual labor.
- Copying global SaaS positioning without Polish market adaptation.
- Recommending a build before a paid validation test.
