# Poland Market Adaptation

## Overview

Global proof is a starting point, not validation. Adapt the niche to Polish buyers, language, trust patterns, pricing, and distribution.

## Adaptation Checklist

Evaluate:

- buyer segment in Poland
- Polish-language workflow needs
- local budget maturity and acceptable price
- trust barriers and proof required
- preferred channels: YouTube, LinkedIn, Facebook groups, Skool, partners, outbound
- local competitors and substitutes
- platform, legal, or data risks
- whether the problem is urgent enough for SMBs

## Scoring

| Factor | What 5 Means |
| --- | --- |
| Local buyer clarity | Specific Polish buyer can be reached and sold |
| Budget fit | Price fits local willingness to pay |
| Language advantage | Polish language/local context creates a wedge |
| Channel fit | We can reach the buyer through our audience or repeatable outbound |
| Trust path | Clear demo, case study, or audit can overcome skepticism |

## Workflow

1. Start from global paid-intent evidence.
2. Identify what transfers directly and what needs localization.
3. Translate the buyer, pain, offer, and proof into Polish-market terms.
4. Define the first local validation test.
5. Decide `build`, `test`, `watch`, or `reject`.

## Output

| Global Pattern | Polish Buyer | Local Pain | Needed Adaptation | Pricing Fit | Channel | Trust Proof | Risk | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

End with:

- first Polish validation test
- minimum proof needed before building
- suggested Polish offer wording
- reason the global idea might fail locally

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming US SaaS pricing transfers to Poland.
- Ignoring Polish language quality and trust.
- Selling AI novelty instead of business outcomes.
- Choosing niches that require enterprise sales before proof exists.
