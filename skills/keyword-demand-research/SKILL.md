# Keyword Demand Research

## Overview

Map what people search for, why they search, and where the query sits in the buying journey. Keywords show attention and intent; they do not prove payment by themselves.

## Query Patterns

Build clusters around:

- `AI for <buyer/job>`
- `automate <workflow>`
- `best tool for <problem>`
- `<competitor> alternative`
- `<tool/category> pricing`
- `how to get more <business outcome>`
- `template`, `calculator`, `audit`, `checklist`
- Polish equivalents and local phrasing when adapting to Poland

## Intent Levels

| Level | Signal | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High | Purchase, pricing, alternative, agency, implementation | "best AI lead follow up software pricing" |
| Medium | Solution search, tool comparison, workflow automation | "automate YouTube clips to newsletter" |
| Low | Education, curiosity, generic trend | "what is AI marketing" |

## Workflow

1. Define buyer and job-to-be-done.
2. Generate seed terms in English and Polish where relevant.
3. Cluster keywords by problem, buyer stage, and intent level.
4. Mark which clusters can become YouTube videos, lead magnets, or landing pages.
5. Send high-intent clusters to `paid-intent-research`.

## Output

| Cluster | Buyer | Example Queries | Intent | Pain | Content Angle | Offer Test | Missing Proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

End with:

- 5 highest-intent queries
- 3 YouTube validation videos
- 1 lead magnet hypothesis
- what paid-intent evidence is still missing

## Common Mistakes

- Ranking only by volume.
- Ignoring "pricing", "alternative", "agency", and "implementation" searches.
- Mixing Polish and global queries without saying which market each query validates.
- Treating keywords as proof that people will buy.
